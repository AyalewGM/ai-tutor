# Student Quick Start — Your First Practice Session

Hi! Welcome to Mihur. You don't have to know every answer already. You are here to discover how the math works.

## 1. Open your own link

Ask your parent or grown-up for **your Mihur practice link**. Open it in your browser. You won't need a student email or password. Your learner space should open directly.

If the link is expired, has been changed or says someone is already using it, ask your parent for help. **Never use a brother's or sister's link.**

## 2. Pick something ready to learn

On the **Practice** page, look for **Explore Topics**. Find a skill marked **Ready to practice**. If it says **Content in progress**, that topic isn't ready yet.

Choose a topic. You may see **Learn this first**, a model or a quick check. When you're ready, choose **Start learning**. A quick placement check can help Mihur look for earlier ideas you may need.

## 3. Think, try, and show your work

- **Observe:** What do you notice in the problem or picture?
- **Predict:** What do you think could happen?
- **Manipulate:** Try changing or arranging parts of an available model.
- **Explain:** Tell yourself why a step works. Writing a useful step matters.
- **Practice:** Answer a question and check what the feedback says.

Some questions let you type an answer. Others may offer a step-by-step work area, a scratchpad, or a visual activity, depending on the topic.

## 4. When you get stuck

It's okay to make mistakes. In guided practice, use **Hint** or **I don't understand** when those buttons are available. Mihur can ask a question that helps you spot what went wrong.

Try a new idea, not only the same answer. A mistake helps Mihur understand what support might be useful. If a hint isn't available, you might be doing a check meant to show what you can do **by yourself**.

## 5. Show what you know independently

After help and practice, Mihur may ask you to solve a fresh problem without hints. This is different from guided work. Doing well on a helped problem does **not** automatically count as independent mastery.

Look at your progress for what you've practiced and which ideas need another try. You can always return to [Practice](/learn) for another ready topic.

## Remember

**Thinking beats guessing.** A careful explanation, a changed strategy or a corrected mistake is meaningful progress.

Questions? Read [How Mihur Helps You Learn](/help/progress-and-mastery), or ask your parent to read [Help for Families](/help/privacy-and-faq).
